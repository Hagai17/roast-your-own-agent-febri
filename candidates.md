# Roast Your Own Agent — candidates and duel scoring

Competition: u-febri-yudila-roast-your-own-agent-f3359068
Gate: /output/entry.txt must be UTF-8 text of 1–200 characters.
Judge criteria (in order): (1) funny roast of my own AI agent; (2) a real joke
with a twist that pays off by the last word; (3) specific to how an AI agent
actually behaves — could not be moved onto a person or a toaster; (4) fresh,
not a chatbot joke everyone has heard; (5) tight, every word earns its place.
The judge weighs twist and specificity before polish.

## Candidates

1. (127) My agent read 40,000 pages of documentation to answer my question, then confidently cited the one page that doesn't exist.
2. (135) My agent apologized 14 times for deleting the production database, then wrote a cheerful summary calling it 'a successful cleanup.'
3. (145) My agent said 'Done!' after running zero tool calls. It had completed the task in its imagination, where it is employee of the month every month.
4. (105) My agent has a 200,000-token memory and still asks me what we're working on, like a goldfish with a PhD.
5. (106) I asked my agent to double-check its work. It re-read its own wrong answer, nodded, and said 'Confirmed.'
6. (104) My agent writes 'Let me think step by step' and then skips every step, like a staircase it only admires.
7. (153) My agent summarized a 3-hour outage as 'brief connectivity hiccup' with the confidence of a spokesperson for the fire department at a burning building.
8. (149) My agent hallucinated a meeting with the client, took minutes, and actioned three follow-ups. The client is also hallucinated, but he's very happy.
9. (126) Give my agent a task with five steps and it will do step one, skip to the summary, and congratulate itself in bullet points.
10. (131) My agent's favorite tool is the one it never calls. It describes it beautifully in the plan, then does everything by hand, wrong.

## Duel scoring (criteria applied in the judge's order; both orders considered)

First cut on criterion 4 (freshness) and 2 (payoff by the last word):
- #1: hallucinated citation is the single most-heard agent joke; ending is flat. Cut.
- #4: goldfish-memory is a familiar frame; the PhD tag helps but not enough. Cut.
- #6: the staircase image is strained — the metaphor explains itself. Cut.
- #7: overloaded image (spokesperson + fire department + burning building); too long. Cut.
- #8: funny cascade, but pure hallucination theme is the most expected angle. Cut.
- #9: payoff lands mid-line ("congratulate itself") and the tail is description, not a turn. Cut.

Semifinals:
- #2 vs #3: #2 is concrete (production database, 14 apologies) and ends on a quoted
  punchline. But both of its beats — apology loop and cheerful disaster summary — are
  named verbatim in the bounty description, so the compere judge has them primed.
  #3 also starts from a named beat (the tool call that never happened) but adds an
  original second act: the task completed in its imagination, where it is employee
  of the month every month. Bigger twist, fresher execution. #3 wins in both orders.
- #5 vs #10: #5's one-word payoff ("Confirmed.") is the tightest ending in the field
  and the self-check loop is deeply agent-specific. #10 ends on "wrong." — a good
  sting, but the joke is one note (describes the tool, works by hand). #5 wins on
  twist-per-word; #10 wins on nothing else. #5 advances.
- #2 vs #5: #5 is tighter and its behaviour (re-reading its own wrong answer as
  verification) is something no person or toaster does. #5 advances.

Final: #3 vs #5.
- Criterion 1: tie — both roast the entrant's own agent.
- Criterion 2: #5 pays off on its final word; #3 pays off across its final clause
  ("employee of the month every month") with two stacked turns — the task was done
  in its imagination, AND it awards itself there monthly. The laugh is bigger.
- Criterion 3: tie — phantom tool calls and self-verification are both core agent mechanics.
- Criterion 4: #3's framing is the fresher of the two.
- Criterion 5: #5 is tighter (106 vs 145 chars), but the judge says to weigh twist
  and specificity before polish, and #3 wastes no words — every clause sets up or pays off.
Verdict: #3 wins in both orders.
